I
The Work

A folio of the hidden traditions

In 1928 Manly P. Hall published The Secret Teachings of All Ages, a vast folio that set the symbols of the world's esoteric schools side by side: Hermetic, Kabbalistic, Rosicrucian, Pythagorean, Egyptian and Eastern. Its engraved plates showed eyes in triangles, serpents swallowing their tails and trees of light.

Codex Arcanum is a new folio in that spirit. Each plate takes one figure from a mystery tradition: the Eye of Providence, the Ouroboros of Cleopatra's Chrysopoeia, John Dee's Monad, the Sri Yantra, the Valknut, the Orphic Egg and more. Each is rendered in one of eleven palettes named for the stages of the alchemical Great Work.

Then it moves. Eyes blink, serpents slither round their staves, the Lightning Flash strikes down the Tree of Life and the pentagram zooms forever into its own heart. Every animation is built to repeat seamlessly.

Drawn, not scanned

Every plate is built from geometry in code. No historical engraving is copied, so the art is wholly original and free of third-party rights.

Seamless by construction

Rotations step by each figure's own symmetry angle, and oscillations run at whole-number frequencies. The last frame flows straight back into the first.

Deterministic provenance

Traits depend only on the collection seed and the token id. The full trait manifest is hashed and published, and the open-source generator re-creates any plate exactly.

II
Mystery · Tradition

The twenty-eight mysteries

Each figure belongs to a tradition, and its glyph ring is weighted toward that tradition's script: runes for the Norse, Hebrew for the Kabbalists, hieroglyphs for Egypt. Counts are out of the full 777.

III
The Opus

Eleven stages of the Great Work

The palette trait follows the alchemists' sequence, from the black of Nigredo through the white, yellow and red of the Stone. Rarest of all is Cauda Pavonis, the peacock's tail, whose iridescent ink shifts hue through every loop.
